Delivery Leadership

Before judging performance, understand the problem

A delivery leadership lesson on understanding performance concerns and creating the conditions for people to succeed.

Published on [publication date] by JCX360

Sometimes people do not need replacing. They need the right support to succeed.

Look beyond the performance

In delivery, it is easy to focus on missed expectations and question whether someone has the right skills for the job.

But there is another question worth asking.

Have we given that person the right support to succeed?

I experienced this while working with an engineer who was facing performance concerns. There had been discussions about whether a change in resource allocation was needed.

When I joined the project, I took time to understand what was happening on the ground.

The issue was not simply capability. The engineer was handling client discussions without enough support and needed more confidence in expressing views.

Sometimes, the environment needs to change

I started joining client calls, providing support and helping create an environment where the engineer could contribute with greater confidence.

Over time, the difference became clear.

The engineer started performing strongly, taking ownership and progressing independently. Delivery moved to two weeks ahead of schedule, without the need for repeated follow-ups or pressure.

That was the real turning point. The improvement was not just in performance. It was visible in the delivery itself.

The experience reinforced something important for me. Before making a decision about someone’s capability, we need to understand the circumstances affecting performance.

Leadership is also about creating the right conditions

Skills matter. Accountability matters. Delivery commitments matter.

But so do guidance, confidence, clear expectations and the support people receive when facing challenges.

Sometimes, a change in personnel is necessary. At other times, the right leadership support can unlock performance that was already there.

The responsibility of a delivery leader is to understand which situation they are dealing with before deciding what action to take.

The takeaway

Before asking whether someone is capable of delivering, ask whether they have the support they need to succeed.

You may discover that the capability was there all along. What was missing was the environment in which that capability could come through.

That is a lesson I continue to carry into delivery leadership.
