Architecture approval is not production readiness

Why an approved design does not guarantee a production-ready solution.

Published on October 12, 2026 by JCX360

Overview

Architecture approval confirms that a proposed solution aligns with agreed architecture principles and technical standards. Production readiness goes further. It checks whether the implemented solution can operate reliably, securely and with clear ownership.

An approved design is a milestone, not a go live decision.

Key takeaway

Architecture approval validates the design. Production readiness validates the solution and its operating model.

What needs to happen before production?

1. Implementation and testing

Confirm that the solution meets functional and non-functional requirements, including performance, resilience and integration behaviour.

2. Security and controls

Verify access permissions, data protection, audit logging and required security controls in the target environment.

3. Monitoring and recovery

Ensure that failures can be detected, investigated and recovered from. Validate deployment and rollback procedures where appropriate.

Important

A solution can follow approved architecture and still fail in production because of untested dependencies, missing monitoring or unclear support ownership.

4. Operational readiness

Confirm support arrangements, escalation paths, runbooks, business readiness and ownership after go live.

5. Release decision

Review outstanding risks, readiness evidence and any architecture conditions. The authorised stakeholders must make the go or no go decision.

The delivery perspective

The solution architect helps maintain design integrity and address architecture conditions. The delivery lead coordinates dependencies, readiness activities, risks and evidence across engineering, testing, security, business and operations.

Production readiness should be planned from the beginning, not left until the final days before release.

The bigger lesson

Architecture approval confirms that the design is acceptable. Production readiness establishes whether the solution is prepared for live operation.

Key takeaway

The objective is not simply to approve and deploy a solution, but to deliver one that can be operated, supported and maintained reliably.
