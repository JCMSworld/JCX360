Supporting AI in production: beyond deployment

Deploying an AI solution is one milestone. Making it sustainable in production is another challenge.

Published on [publication date] by JCX360

Production is not the end of delivery

In many technology programmes, the focus is on building solutions, completing testing and getting them into production.

But one important question often comes later.

Who will support these solutions once they are live?

Moving a solution into production also introduces operational responsibilities. Someone needs to manage incidents, monitor performance, maintain integrations and ensure the service continues to operate as expected.

Without a clear support model, these responsibilities often remain with the development team.

What happens when you have no baseline data?

This is where another challenge starts.

How do you know how many resources are required when you have no historical incident data, support effort or operational workload?

A low number of reported incidents does not necessarily mean low support demand. Monitoring, maintenance, access requests, data issues and informal support activities may never be recorded as incidents.

Without a baseline, resource planning becomes an exercise based on assumptions rather than evidence.

You risk overestimating the support requirement, underestimating operational demand or creating unnecessary dependency on the development team.

Start with evidence, not assumptions

When historical data is unavailable, waiting for it is not the answer.

A practical approach is to establish an initial support arrangement, capture operational activities and measure the actual workload over an agreed period.

Some of the key measures include:

  • Incident volumes, severity and resolution effort.
  • Application and platform monitoring.
  • Data and integration failures.
  • Maintenance and service requests.
  • Release and production support.
  • Development effort diverted to production issues.

This creates an operational baseline that can be used to review resource requirements and improve the support model over time.

Support starts with architecture

Modern AI solutions are not isolated components. They depend on applications, integrations, data sources, orchestration and underlying platform services.

An issue reported at the application level may actually originate in one of these dependencies.

That is why supportability should be considered during solution design, not after deployment.

Monitoring, logging, clear ownership, operational documentation and escalation paths are important parts of production readiness.

The takeaway

AI delivery does not end at deployment.

Building a solution is one responsibility. Operating, supporting and continuously improving it is another.

When you have no baseline, start measuring. When you have evidence, use it to make better decisions.

That is how we move from delivering AI solutions to sustaining reliable enterprise services.
