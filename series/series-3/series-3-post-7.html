AI Reliability Engineering: When Models Become Production Dependencies

Keeping business outcomes reliable when AI models are unavailable, uncertain or produce incorrect answers.

Published on [publication date] by JCX360

Once AI becomes part of a critical business process, something changes.

The model becomes a production dependency.

And production dependencies fail.

Models can become unavailable.

Responses can become slower.

Rate limits can be reached.

External services can fail.

And sometimes the model is available, but the answer is simply not good enough.

This means AI systems need reliability engineering too.

We need to think about:

  • SLIs and SLOs
  • Latency
  • Retries and fallbacks
  • Graceful degradation
  • Monitoring
  • Incident response
  • Cost controls

But AI introduces an additional question.

In a traditional application:

Service fails → retry or fallback

In an AI application:

Model fails → what happens to the business decision?

That is a very different problem.

Reliability principle

Reliability is not just about keeping the model available. It is about keeping the business outcome reliable when the model is unavailable, uncertain, or wrong.

That is where AI engineering and SRE genuinely meet.
